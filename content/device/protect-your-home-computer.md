## How to Protect Your Home Computer Without Being a Tech Expert

Knowing how to protect your home computer does not require technical skills. Most problems start with small mistakes, like clicking the wrong link or skipping updates. A few simple habits can keep your computer safe and working properly.

This page gives clear, easy tips you can use yourself or share with family members.

## Keep Your Computer Updated

Updates fix problems that attackers use to break into computers.

Make sure you:

- Turn on automatic updates
- Restart your computer when updates are ready
- Update browsers and important apps

<Tip>If your computer asks to update, let it.</Tip>

Outdated software is one of the biggest reasons computers get infected.

## Use Antivirus and Security Tools

Basic security software helps block viruses and unsafe files.

Good habits include:

- Using a trusted antivirus program
- Keeping it turned on
- Letting it run regular scans

<Info>Most computers already include basic security tools. You just need to keep them active.</Info>

Security software works quietly in the background to protect your system.

## Be Careful What You Download

Many computer problems start with unsafe downloads.

Avoid:

- Free software from unknown websites
- Files sent by strangers
- “Cracked” or pirated programs

<Warning>A single bad download can slow down or damage your computer.</Warning>

If you do not trust the source, do not download the file.

## Watch Out for Fake Alerts

Some websites show fake warnings that claim your computer has a virus. They try to scare you into clicking or paying.

These fake alerts often:

- Flash red warnings
- Play loud sounds
- Ask you to call a number

<DoDont doText="Close the browser tab if you see a scary pop-up." dontText="Call numbers or click buttons on fake warning screens." />

Real security alerts do not behave this way.

## Use Strong Login Passwords

Your computer account should have a strong password.

Use passwords that:

- Are long
- Are hard to guess
- Are not shared with other accounts

<Tip>If someone can log into your computer, they can do almost anything.</Tip>

A good password helps keep others out.

## Back Up Important Files

Backups save you when something goes wrong.

You should:

- Back up photos and documents
- Use an external drive or cloud storage
- Check backups from time to time

<Info>Backups protect you from accidents, viruses, and hardware failure.</Info>

If your computer breaks, your files do not have to be lost.

## Keep Your Home Network Safe

Your computer connects to the internet through your home network.

Basic steps:

- Set a strong Wi-Fi password
- Do not share it with strangers
- Keep your router updated

<DoDont doText="Use a strong password on your home Wi-Fi." dontText="Leave your network open or unprotected." />

A safe network helps protect every device in your home.

## Learn to Spot Unsafe Behavior

Many computer problems come from unsafe actions, not broken software.

Watch out for:

- Clicking unknown links
- Opening strange attachments
- Giving control of your computer to others

<Quote>“Most computer problems start with one bad click.”</Quote>

Being careful online protects your computer as much as any tool.
